Getting Started with Digital Garden

A comprehensive guide to building your personal research archive and publishing your first article.

🕐 8 min read
📅 January 20, 2024
Guide Research

Contents

    What is Digital Garden?

    A Digital Garden is a personal knowledge management system—a space where you cultivate your ideas, research, and explorations. Unlike a blog with chronological posts, a garden grows organically, with ideas interconnected and evolving over time.

    This particular Digital Garden is designed to showcase your breadth of knowledge and systematic thinking across multiple disciplines: technology, design, philosophy, personal development, and beyond.

    Why Create One?

    For students and professionals, a Digital Garden demonstrates:

    • Intellectual depth — You engage deeply with complex ideas
    • Breadth of knowledge — You understand connections across disciplines
    • Communication skills — You can explain ideas clearly to others
    • Technical capability — You can build and maintain web projects
    • Self-direction — You drive your own learning and research

    This is exactly what universities and employers look for in candidates.

    Getting Set Up

    Prerequisites

    You need:

    • A GitHub account (free)
    • A text editor (VS Code, Sublime, Notepad++, etc.)
    • Basic understanding of HTML and CSS (or willingness to learn)
    • No command line required!

    Step 1: Create a GitHub Repository

    Go to github.com/new and create a repository called digital-garden. Make it public so others can see your work.

    Step 2: Upload the Files

    Download the Digital Garden template files. You can either:

    • Clone the repository (if you know Git)
    • Download as ZIP and extract
    • Upload files one by one through GitHub's web interface

    Step 3: Enable GitHub Pages

    In your repository settings:

    1. Go to Settings → Pages
    2. Select "Deploy from a branch"
    3. Choose "main" branch
    4. Your site will be live at username.github.io/digital-garden

    Creating Your First Article

    Copy the Template

    Open articles/article-template.html and save it as articles/my-first-article.html

    Edit the Frontmatter

    At the top of the file, you'll see a comment with metadata: 

    <!--
FRONTMATTER:
title: "Your Article Title"
description: "Brief description for the home page"
date: "2024-01-20"
categories: ["IT", "Design"]
tags: ["system", "thinking"]
status: "Published"
-->

    Fill this in with your article's information.

    Write Your Content

    The template has sections for:

    • Introduction
    • Main content sections
    • Code examples
    • Images (click to expand)
    • Conclusion

    Use standard HTML tags: <h2>, <p>, <ul>, <blockquote>, etc.

    Add It to the Home Page

    Edit index.html and add your article to the data structure. The articles list is a JavaScript array that you update manually.

    Article Best Practices

    Structure

    Good articles follow this structure:

    1. Hook — Grab attention in the first paragraph
    2. Problem — What are you exploring and why does it matter?
    3. Analysis — Deep dive into the topic
    4. Examples — Show concrete cases or code
    5. Takeaway — What did we learn? What's next?

    Writing Tips

    • Write for clarity, not for showing off
    • Use short sentences and paragraphs
    • Include examples and references
    • Link to related articles when relevant
    • Don't worry about perfection — you can update articles anytime

    Images

    Store images in the images/ folder. Readers can click to view them fullscreen. Use high-quality images.

    Publishing Your Article

    1. Save your article file
    2. Update index.html with the article metadata
    3. Commit and push to GitHub
    4. GitHub Pages will rebuild automatically (takes ~1 minute)
    5. Your article is live!

    Categories and Organization

    Start with these broad categories:

    • IT & Technology
    • Design & UX
    • Philosophy & Theory
    • Personal Development
    • Life & Reflections

    Articles can belong to multiple categories. Over time, patterns will emerge and you can refine or expand your system.

    Customization

    Change the Title and Description

    Edit the <title> and <meta name="description"> tags in index.html

    Add Your Links

    Update the footer with your real email, LinkedIn, and GitHub in both index.html and articles/article-template.html

    Change Colors

    Edit css/theme.css to change the color variables. The system supports light and dark modes automatically.

    Conclusion

    Your Digital Garden is ready to grow. Start with one article about something you're passionate about. Build from there. Over time, you'll have a powerful portfolio that demonstrates your thinking, breadth, and depth of knowledge.

    Remember: this isn't about perfection. It's about sharing your genuine explorations and helping others see how you think.

    "A garden requires patient labor and attention. Plants are living things and they respond to care." — Gertrude Jekyll

    Happy gardening! 🌱
